# [Looking for collaborators] X-P1: a DIY low-cost clamshell 4G Linux/Android phone with a keyboard and gaming controls — would you build one?

Hello makers, open-hardware enthusiasts and Linux/Android developers! We're proposing **X-P1**, a foldable phone inspired by 1990s electronic translators, with a very practical goal: **publish enough documentation that anyone could order parts online, manufacture the custom PCB and enclosure through a service such as PCBWay (or print locally), and assemble the device themselves.**

**Concept renders pending upload.**

### Intended features

- **Working 4G phone**: calls, SMS and data, including **answering calls while folded** through a touch E-Paper external display, speaker and microphone.
- Linux ARM and possibly Android; QWERTY keyboard, recessed dual analog sticks (L3/R3), side scroll wheel, and four rear shoulder buttons **on the lower base, NOT on the display lid or hinge spine**.
- Touch internal IPS display (~4″), exterior touch E-Paper (~4.7″), inner/outer cameras, stereo speakers along the interior screen, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, FM radio, infrared and USB-C.
- **Protected removable 7,000 mAh battery pack** with independent service access, plus a removable **M.2 2230 NVMe SSD** under a separate rear hatch.
- Gray aluminum/plastic and black/orange finishes; **same hardware geometry**.

We're exploring a **Radxa CM5 RK3588S2 with 8 GB RAM** and **SIMCom SIM7600G-H** modem. Current size target is roughly **163 × 100 × 30–36 mm folded**. These are **unverified concept targets**: no PCB, production CAD, working prototype, confirmed battery fit, Android telephony support, or final costs yet. The images are concept art generated with AI—not production drawings.

### Would you join?

We'd love feedback from **KiCad/PCB and power/battery engineers; embedded Linux, Android BSP and VoLTE developers; RF/antenna specialists; CAD/hinge and flex cable designers; screen/audio/camera specialists; procurement/manufacturing and hands-on builders**.

**Please tell us:** (1) would you build it if source files and instructions were public? (2) what could you help with? (3) which features should be optional in a cheaper v1? (4) what is a realistic all-in cost? (5) do you know existing modules and manufacturers that could simplify the project?

This is an **interest check and request for technical collaboration**. **No donations, sales or component purchases are requested.** We want to discover whether a small open-source community can turn the idea into a tested, reproducible build.
